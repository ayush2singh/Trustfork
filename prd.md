# 05. TrustFork — Post-Partition Authorization Reconciliation

**05. TrustFork — Post-Partition Authorization Reconciliation**
**Problem Statement**
During a network partition, separate parts of a distributed authorization system may continue making bounded access decisions using local policy state, even as an authoritative policy update occurs on one side. When connectivity returns, the problem is not choosing the latest decision but determining which partition-time decisions were valid under the machine-readable policy version that should have governed them and correcting any effects produced by decisions that are no longer defensible. Design a system that simulates two partitioned authorization domains, records every decision with the policy evidence available at that moment, and reconciles them against an explicit authoritative policy history after reconnection. The system must distinguish a locally reasonable decision that remains valid from one that must be revoked or compensated, without relying on natural-language judgment. A convincing demonstration should include conflicting allow/deny outcomes produced during the partition and end with a machine-checkable reconciliation showing which decisions survive, which are invalidated, and that both sides converge to the same authoritative policy state.
